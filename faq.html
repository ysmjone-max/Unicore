Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Key questions and answers organized by student lifecycle stages: pre-arrival applications, current studies in Italy, and post-graduation alumni pathways.

No. The UNICORE application is 100% free of charge. No university, UNHCR office, or partner organization ever charges any fee for applying, taking faculty interviews, or receiving scholarship awards.

Applicants must be recognized refugees residing in one of the 13 designated asylum countries (such as Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Mozambique, Niger, Nigeria, Cameroon, Zambia, Zimbabwe, South Africa, Bangladesh, or India), hold a recognized Bachelor's degree (minimum GPA comparable to Italian 24/30 or 65%), and have obtained their degree in or after 2021.

Under official UNICORE guidelines, each candidate is allowed to apply to a maximum of two (2) Italian universities per edition cycle. If an applicant submits applications to more than two universities, their applications may be disqualified.

Recognizing that refugee students often cannot obtain a Declaration of Value (*Dichiarazione di Valore*) from embassies in their country of origin, all UNICORE universities accept alternative documentation, including CIMEA Statements of Comparability and Verification or the European Qualifications Passport for Refugees (EQPR) issued by the Council of Europe.

Most participating Italian universities accept an official letter from your undergraduate university confirming that English was the medium of instruction (EMI). In addition, your English proficiency will be directly assessed during the technical faculty interview.

You can apply using your valid UNHCR refugee registration certificate or national refugee identity card. If you are selected, UNHCR, the Italian Embassy, and local authorities coordinate an emergency consular travel document (*Laissez-Passer*) for your safe entry to Italy.

Scholars receive an initial welcome pocket allowance upon arrival to cover immediate necessities, followed by monthly stipends (typically between €450 and €600 per month) directly transferred into your Italian bank or postal account (*Conto Corrente*).

Within 8 working days of arriving in Italy, you will complete the residence permit application (*Kit Giallo*) at a Poste Italiane Sportello Amico. University international offices and local partners assist you with submitting the kit, purchasing the required revenue stamp (*Marca da Bollo*), and attending your biometric appointment at the local Police Headquarters (*Questura*).

All UNICORE students are enrolled in the Italian National Health Service (*Servizio Sanitario Nazionale - SSN*). Once registered at the local health agency (*ASL*), you receive a *Tessera Sanitaria* and are assigned a local general family doctor (*Medico di Base*) providing free consultations, medical prescriptions, and hospital care.

To renew your scholarship and accommodation for the second academic year, universities typically require earning a specified number of university credits (usually between 30 and 36 CFU/ECTS by August 10th of your first year). Academic tutors are assigned to assist you with exam scheduling and preparation.

Based on the peer mentorship action plan established by senior scholars, incoming students are paired with Italian and senior international student buddies. Buddies assist with campus orientation, opening bank accounts, setting up SIM cards, registering for exams, and navigating student life in your city.

Yes. Italian law permits non-EU students holding a valid *Permesso di Soggiorno Studio* to engage in part-time employment for up to a maximum of 20 hours per week (or up to 1,040 hours per calendar year), provided work commitments do not interfere with maintaining required academic credits.

Upon graduation, Italian immigration law allows Master's degree holders to apply for a 12-month Job Search Residence Permit (*Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro o Imprenditorialità degli Studenti*). Once you secure an employment contract, this permit can be converted into a regular Subordinate Work permit (*Permesso per Lavoro Subordinato*).

Yes. With a fully accredited Italian *Laurea Magistrale* (120 CFU / Second-Cycle Degree), UNICORE graduates meet all academic requirements to compete for fully funded Ph.D. positions and research fellowships across Italian, European, and international universities.

Graduates can join the active UNICORE Students Association on LinkedIn and professional networking channels to mentor incoming cohorts, share job openings, collaborate on research, and participate in annual higher education advocacy panels.
