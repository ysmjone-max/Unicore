Frequently Asked Questions

Find clear, verified answers structured across every stage of the student lifecycle: pre-arrival applications, daily student life in Italy, and post-graduation career opportunities.

No, applying for UNICORE is completely 100% free of charge. UNHCR, participating Italian universities, and partner organizations will never ask for money or payment at any stage of the application or selection process. Anyone asking for money claiming to represent UNICORE is committing fraud.

To be eligible for UNICORE, you must meet all the following criteria:

  • • Be an officially recognized refugee residing in one of the 13 eligible countries (Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ethiopia, India, Kenya, Mozambique, Niger, Nigeria, South Africa, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe).
  • • Hold a Bachelor's degree (equivalent to at least 3.0 / 4.0 GPA) recognized as eligible for entry into an Italian Master's program.
  • • Have obtained your Bachelor's degree no earlier than the year specified in the respective call (usually within the last 5 years).
  • • Have never been enrolled at an Italian university before.

Under UNICORE guidelines, candidates may select and apply to a maximum of two (2) Italian universities among the participating institutions. If you apply to more than two, only the first two submitted applications will be considered, or your dossier may be invalidated.

Many participating Italian universities accept an official English as Medium of Instruction (EMI) certificate from your Bachelor's university if your entire undergraduate degree was taught in English. Additionally, your English proficiency will be evaluated directly during the online interview with the university board.

Upon arrival in Italy and opening an Italian bank account (with an IBAN and Italian *Codice Fiscale*), your university or regional scholarship authority (*Ente Regionale per il Diritto allo Studio*) will disburse your monthly allowance directly into your bank account. Allowances typically range from €450 to €600 per month, alongside free or heavily subsidized dormitory housing and meal cafeteria subsidies.

Within 8 working days of arrival in Italy, you must send the application packet (*Kit Giallo*) from an Italian post office (*Poste Italiane - Sportello Amico*). UNICORE tutors and local partner organizations (such as Centro Astalli or Diaconia Valdese) will assist you with filling out the forms, submitting tax stamps, and scheduling your fingerprinting appointment at the local police headquarters (*Questura*).

Yes. Under Italian immigration law, holders of a *Permesso di Soggiorno per Studio* are legally permitted to work part-time for up to a maximum of 20 hours per week (or up to 1,040 hours per year). However, students must ensure their employment does not interfere with academic exam requirements.

Upon successfully completing your Master’s degree (*Laurea Magistrale*), you are legally entitled to apply for the Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro o Imprenditorialità (Job Search Permit). This permit grants you a 12-month legal residence extension in Italy to search for employment or register a business, which can subsequently be converted into a regular Work Residence Permit (*Permesso per Lavoro Subordinato* or *Autonomo*).

Yes. Your Italian Master’s degree (*Laurea Magistrale*, 120 ECTS) is fully accredited across the European Higher Education Area (EHEA) and recognized worldwide. Numerous UNICORE alumni have successfully entered fully-funded Ph.D. programs and research fellowships in Italy and across the European Union.
