To be eligible for UNICORE 8.0, you must:

  • Be recognized as a refugee residing in one of the 13 designated countries (Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ethiopia, India, Kenya, Mozambique, Niger, Nigeria, South Africa, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe).
  • Hold a valid Bachelor’s degree (minimum 3 years) equivalent to Italian higher education standards.
  • Have a Grade Point Average (GPA) of at least 3.0 (out of 4.0) or national equivalent.
  • Have graduated in or after 2021 (check specific university calls for any variations).
  • Not have been previously enrolled at any UNICORE partner Italian university.

Candidates may submit a maximum of two (2) applications to different Italian universities. Submitting more than two will result in only the first two chronological submissions being considered.

An expensive commercial certificate (such as IELTS or TOEFL) is generally not required if your undergraduate degree was taught in English. Your language proficiency and conversational ability will be directly evaluated during the online interview with the university selection committee.

If your academic documents were lost or incomplete due to forced displacement, notify the admissions department of your target university during application. Partner institutions and CIMEA work with the European Qualifications Passport for Refugees (EQPR) framework to assess background qualifications where appropriate.

The full UNICORE scholarship package provides:

  • 100% Tuition Fee Exemption: Complete university tuition fee waiver.
  • Living Allowance: Monthly stipend for food, local transport, and personal expenses.
  • Accommodation: Free university dormitory room or dedicated housing grant through Caritas/partners.
  • Health Insurance: Full registration in the Italian National Health Service (SSN) with a family doctor.
  • Academic Support: Study materials allowance (laptops/books), Italian language classes, and student peer buddies.

Yes. Under Italian immigration law, international holders of a Student Residence Permit (Permesso di Soggiorno per Studio) are legally permitted to work part-time up to 20 hours per week (or up to 1,040 hours per calendar year).

Scholarship renewal is based on academic achievement. Students must complete and pass a minimum number of university course credits (**CFU - Crediti Formativi Universitari**, typically around 30 CFU by the end of their first academic year) to continue receiving both their living stipend and student residence permit.

Local humanitarian partners (Caritas Italiana, Diaconia Valdese, Centro Astalli) and university international student desks will accompany you in obtaining your Codice Fiscale, submitting your Permesso di Soggiorno application at the Questura/Poste Italiane, registering with the ASL (Health Authority), and opening an Italian bank account.

Yes. Italian law grants graduates of Italian Master’s programs (Laurea Magistrale) the right to convert their study permit into a 12-Month Job Search Residence Permit (Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro o Imprenditorialità). Upon securing employment, this permit can be converted into a standard Work Residence Permit (Permesso di Soggiorno per Lavoro Subordinato).
