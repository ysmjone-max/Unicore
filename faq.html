Frequently Asked Questions

Verified answers addressing key questions from the official UNICORE student surveys, covering pre-arrival preparation, daily campus life in Italy, and post-graduation career opportunities.

No. Applying for UNICORE is completely 100% free of charge. UNHCR, participating Italian universities, and partner organizations will never ask for money or payment at any stage of the application or selection process. Anyone requesting money claiming to represent UNICORE is committing fraud.

To be eligible for UNICORE, you must satisfy all the following criteria:

  • • Be an officially recognized refugee residing in one of the 13 eligible countries of asylum: Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ethiopia, India, Kenya, Mozambique, Niger, Nigeria, South Africa, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe.
  • • Hold a Bachelor's degree (equivalent to at least 3.0 / 4.0 GPA) recognized as eligible for entry into an Italian Master's program.
  • • Have obtained your Bachelor's degree no earlier than the year specified in the respective call (usually within the last 5 years).
  • • Have never been enrolled at an Italian higher education institution before.

Candidates may select and apply to a maximum of two (2) Italian universities among the participating institutions. If you submit applications to more than two universities, only the first two submitted will be evaluated, and additional applications may be cancelled.

Due to difficulties refugees often face in obtaining a traditional Declaration of Value from Italian consulates in their country of origin, participating universities accept the CIMEA Statement of Comparability / Verification or the European Qualifications Passport for Refugees (EQPR) issued by the Council of Europe as official alternative credential evaluations.

Many participating Italian universities accept an official English as Medium of Instruction (EMI) letter from your undergraduate university if your entire degree was taught in English. Additionally, your conversational and academic English proficiency will be tested directly during the virtual faculty interview.

In cases where national authorities in the country of first asylum delay the renewal of Convention Travel Documents (CTD), UNHCR and the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAECI) coordinate with Italian diplomatic missions. In exceptional circumstances, a specialized Laissez-Passer or Italian Titolo di Viaggio per Stranieri may be arranged to ensure safe departure.

Upon arrival in Italy and opening an Italian bank account (with an IBAN and Italian Codice Fiscale), your university or regional scholarship authority (Ente Regionale per il Diritto allo Studio - DSU) will disburse your monthly allowance directly into your bank account. Allowances typically range from €450 to €600 per month, alongside free or heavily subsidized dormitory housing and meal cafeteria subsidies (Mensa).

Within 8 working days of your arrival in Italy, you must submit the application kit (Kit Giallo) at a local post office (Poste Italiane - Sportello Amico). UNICORE student buddies and partner organizations (such as Centro Astalli or Diaconia Valdese) will assist you with filling out the forms, affixing the revenue stamp (Marca da Bollo), and scheduling your fingerprinting appointment at the local police headquarters (Questura).

UNICORE scholars are assisted in registering with the Italian National Health Service (Servizio Sanitario Nazionale - SSN) through their local health agency (ASL). Registration provides you with a Tessera Sanitaria, granting access to a general practitioner (Medico di Base), hospital emergency care, and specialist consultations, with specific fee exemptions applicable to scholarship recipients.

To maintain and renew your scholarship for the second academic year, universities require students to achieve a minimum number of university educational credits (CFU/ECTS) — typically around 30 to 36 ECTS credits by August 10th of the first year. Academic tutors and peer buddies provide continuous support to help you plan exam sessions.

Every newly arrived scholar is paired with a trained Italian student or alumni "Buddy". Buddies assist with navigating campus digital platforms, public transportation cards, setting up Wi-Fi and bank accounts, introducing local cultural customs, and providing conversational Italian language practice.

Yes. Under Italian immigration law, holders of a Permesso di Soggiorno per Studio are legally authorized to work part-time for up to 20 hours per week (up to a maximum of 1,040 hours per calendar year). However, students must ensure part-time work does not negatively affect lecture attendance or exam preparation.

Upon successfully graduating with your Master’s degree (Laurea Magistrale), you are entitled to apply for the Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro o Imprenditorialità (Job Search Residence Permit). This grants you a 12-month legal extension in Italy to search for employment or register a startup, which can then be converted into a regular Work Residence Permit (Permesso per Lavoro Subordinato or Autonomo).

Yes. Your Italian Master’s degree (Laurea Magistrale, 120 ECTS) is fully accredited across the European Higher Education Area (EHEA) and recognized worldwide. Numerous UNICORE alumni have successfully entered fully-funded Ph.D. programs and research fellowships in Italy and across the European Union.

All UNICORE graduates are welcomed into the UNICORE Alumni Network. The network facilitates career mentorship, professional networking with Italian and European corporate partners (such as Banca IFIS, humanitarian institutes, and research centers), and allows alumni to mentor newly arriving cohorts of scholars.
