Roadmap & Process

How UNICORE Works & How It Is Organised

A transparent, merit-based 5-stage pathway from initial online application to arrival in Italy, degree completion, and post-graduation career opportunities.

The 5 Stages

The Complete Scholar Journey

Each phase is accompanied by university academic coordinators and humanitarian partner organizations.

1
March – April Phase 1: Application

Online Application & Refugee Screening

Candidates consult the 430+ courses offered by the 40 partner universities. Each student may apply to a maximum of 2 universities through the centralized portal. Applications require undergraduate degree certificates, transcripts, CV, motivation letters, and refugee documentation.

  • Bachelor's Degree (GPA ≥ 3.0 or equivalent)
  • Official UNHCR Refugee ID Card
  • Max 2 University Applications
  • 100% Free Application Process
2
April – May Phase 2: Selection

Document Merit Evaluation & Online Interview

Independent university committees evaluate candidates through a strict two-tier scoring process:

Step 1: Document Review (0–40 points) — Academic background, grades, CV, and subject alignment (Minimum passing score: 25 pts).
Step 2: Virtual Interview (0–40 points) — Technical competence, academic passion, and English language proficiency.
  • Merit-based Evaluation by Professors
  • Live Interview on MS Teams / Zoom
  • Anonymous Result Publication (Refugee ID)
  • Direct Email Notification to Winners
3
June – August Phase 3: Pre-Departure

Study Visa Issuance, Flight Ticketing & Briefings

Admitted scholars receive direct administrative support for their Italian National Study Visa (Type D) through Italian Embassies and MAECI. UNHCR and local partners coordinate CIMEA Comparability certificates, travel documents, pre-departure webinars, and flight tickets to Italy.

  • National Study Visa (Type D) Assistance
  • Flight Tickets Covered by Programme
  • CIMEA Degree Equivalence Statement
  • Cultural & Logistics Pre-departure Webinar
4
September – Year 2 Phase 4: Studies in Italy

Arrival, Academic Studies & Local Integration

Scholars arrive in Italy and are welcomed by university international offices and local partners (Caritas, Diaconia Valdese, Centro Astalli). Support covers housing placement, Codice Fiscale, Permesso di Soggiorno, National Health Service (SSN) enrollment, monthly stipends, and student peer buddies.

  • Housing & Living Stipend Distribution
  • Permesso di Soggiorno & Healthcare Setup
  • Student Peer Buddy Integration
  • Legal Part-time Work Rights (up to 20h/week)
5
Post-Graduation Phase 5: Career & Alumni

Degree Completion, Job Search Permit & Career Pathways

Upon completing their Laurea Magistrale, graduates are legally eligible under Italian immigration law to convert their study permit into a 12-Month Job Search Residence Permit (Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro), pursue doctoral research (Ph.D.), or transition directly into full-time employment in Europe.

  • 12-Month Job Search Permit Conversion
  • Subordinate Work Permit Transition
  • Funded Ph.D. Research Opportunities
  • UNICORE Alumni Mentorship Network
Eligibility Criteria

Standard Candidate Admission Requirements

While each university issues its own official call for applications, the following criteria apply across the UNICORE framework.

Mandatory Qualifications

  • Refugee Status: Recognized as a refugee residing in one of the 13 eligible countries.
  • Academic Degree: Bachelor's degree (minimum 3 years) from a recognized university.
  • Grade Point Average (GPA): Minimum 3.0 out of 4.0 (or regional equivalent conversion).
  • Graduation Timeline: Degree obtained in or after 2021 (some universities have no limit).
  • First-time Candidate: Never previously enrolled at a UNICORE partner institution.

Required Application Dossier

  • Refugee Documentation: Proof of refugee status / ration card.
  • Degree Certificate & Transcripts: Official transcripts showing all completed coursework.
  • Curriculum Vitae (CV): Detailed academic and professional background.
  • Motivation Letter: Explanation of academic goals and program choice.
  • Reference Letters: Academic recommendation letters (where requested).
