UNICORE SURVEYS  
Students, Universities and Local Partners 
UNICORE 1.0-4.0 surveys have been conducted in order to better understand where UNHCR  
meeting refugee students, universities and local partners’ needs and where we have 
opportunities to improve and to strengthen the way we engage and collaborate focusing on 
participation, level of appreciation and empowerment. 
UNHCR launched two concurred surveys, Students and Universities & local Partners, at the 
beginning of February 2023 and they run 5 working days. The target groups were students (103), 
universities (38) and local partners  - invited by the universities  - from the 4 editions of the 
project. 
Students’ Survey 
 
 
 
 
81 students (83,4% of invitees) contributed to the survey representing the 4 UNICORE’s 
editionsi. 
They expressed a good level of appreciation  of the project reporting 6 to 8 points’ average 
concerning the main phases of the project ii and comfort of the physical environment. Students 
shared concerns for the documentation (TD, visa, residence permit, etc.) (37), financial 
support (21) and dissemination (16) of the project through the refugee population, as main 
areas of improvement for the project, followed by support in the integration in the receiving 
community (15), academic and orientation tutorship (14) and psychosocial support (12). In 
particular arrive early before class at list it would be extremely important to have one month 
integration time for the beginning of courses and need of Acquiring of Travelling document have 
been explicitly mentioned to this end. 
Vast majority of responders confirmed to have fully understood the objective of UNICORE and to 
have received means and resources adequate to carry out their study (61) showing a good level 
of support and listening in the hosting environment in terms of ease to inform the university and 
the UNICORE project partners about problems and difficulties encountered in the study or in the 
inclusion process (67).

However, some students reported delays in receiving the courses material (2) and laptop (1) and 
to have a weak internet connection (1), impacting on the possibility to study properly. 
For what concerns relationships within university and local communities 63 students expressed 
good level of appreciation while 3 reported to not agree (2) and strongly not agree (1), while 
remaining neutral in 15 cases. In particular, it has been observed that There is no good 
cooperation in the university with the colleagues, the cooperation is very low, because they don't 
approach you as a new person, it is an individual who have to approach (1). 
The state of conflictuality and in particular the perception of bully dynamics and unfair behaviors 
against refugee students has been perceived by 12 responders (9 agree, 3 strongly agree), 
complementing with further details reporting rudeness from local partner operators (1), specific 
bully episodes (3) and discriminatory language and attitude s in the residencies where students 
are accommodated and employing institutions. 
 In 50 cases students reported to feel properly involved in decisions process concerning their stay 
in Italy, while in the remaining cases they reported neutrality (23), to disagree (5) and to strongly 
disagree (3). Some of them highlighted the need to be involved in ad hoc meeting; to be assisted 
in the asylum procedure and to be informed about post-graduation possibilities. 
For what concerns UNICORE 4.0 students, they showed good level of appreciation on information 
received on the terms of the project and of the scholarship’s opportunity, provided in a clear, 
accessible and exhaustive way (40 out of 43). 
In 79 cases (out of 81) UNICORE students would recommend this opportunity to other refugees 
seeking scholarship opportunities in the future and 80 would recommend the expansion of 
UNICORE projects to other countries in Europe, showing a significant overall level of appreciation 
for the project. 
It’s worth to note that the surv ey showed to be an important collector of input provided by the 
students in particular for what concern the proposal to create a UNICORE students’ committee 
(36 comments), in order to strengthen the participation and involvement of students in the project, 
and general consideration and suggestion to ameliorate the project (43 comments).  
Students expressed preference in starting consultation for the committee  through a Teams’ 
call and they reported the need to further explore possibilities to ensure their regular involvement 
for instance combining an annual in presence meeting with regular on-line consultations, including 
Whatsapp group, and implementing a representation system involving one student from each 
region where UNICORE scholarships are offered. Th e committee has been also considered in 
terms of facilitating the dissemination of information within the concerned refugee population in 
order ensure that potential eligible candidates have effective access to relevant information, 
selection process and transparent procedures. The committee has been also proposed to support 
the orientation of new selected students to facilitate their inclusion in Italy and in general to actively 
contribute to the decision -making process of the refugee students’ population interested in 
continuing their studies with UNICORE.

They expressed a good level of appreciation  of the project reporting that their expectations on 
UNICORE have been met (41 cases - 86% of the responders). For what concerns coordination

Concluding remarks 
Both surveys show a good level of appreciation for the UNICORE project as regards quantitative and 
qualitative aspects concerning participation, level of appreciation and empowerment. 
However, there are a range of different expectations and needs that were captured in the surveys’ results. 
In particular, considering the significant number of narrative inputs shared by the refugee students, it 
emerges a strong willingness to actively participate to the different phases of  the project  in order to 
ensure that their views and consideration are further taken into consideration and in a perspective of 
continuous amelioration of the project. 
As the results demonstrate, notwithstanding the reported good level of inclusion, it’s worth to note that 
12 students (over 81) reported perceived bully dynamics and unfair behaviors revealing need of further 
strengthen efforts at local level to ensure that trusted communication channels with UNICORE 
beneficiaries are created, reinforced and maintained in order to immediately react on eventual 
discriminatory behaviors and to fully implement a zero tolerance approach against harassment or 
discrimination and contribute to enhance a inclusive local society. 
It is encouraging that most universities and local partners expressed an average level of appreciation for 
the project. However, it is important to note that most respondents shared concerns regarding countries 
of departure and pre-departure and post-arrival documentation. Building a further detailed list of criteria 
in the identification of countries of departure, guided by best practices, lessons learned and relevant

Recommendations 
The key recommendation leading from the UNICORE surveys and objectives are related to the main areas 
observed, as follows: 
➢ Establish a UNICORE students committee  in accordance with their needs and preferred 
modalities and in coordination with UNICORE’s partners in order to ensure a refugee -centered 
approach of the project; 
➢ Facilitating necessary language training at no cost inter alia for the crucial role played b y the 
language in the inclusion mutual process; 
➢ Facilitating training to universities and local partners to improve knowledge and networking with 
existing inclusion possibilities at local and national level; 
➢ Ensuring that refugee students have access to un iversity or provider services, support, and 
activities such as career placement, social organisations, internships, extracurricular activities, 
etc. in order to ensure a vast spectrum of effective post-graduation options; 
➢ Providing and/or facilitating acce ss to academic and psychosocial support, where necessary, to 
encourage student retention, success and to ensure that eventually discrimination and 
harassment episodes are properly managed.

Background1 
In the November 2016 New York Declaration on Refugees and Migrants (New York Declaration), 
States Parties committed to expanding opportunities for refugees and persons in need of 
international protection to enter a third country through safe and legal chan nels of entry, 
adhering to resettlement programs and promoting complementary pathways to entry and 
protection. 
The New York Declaration further articulates the commitment to develop humanitarian 
pathways and increase opportunities for refugees to obtain study visas or access scholarships in 
European Union member states. 
On Dec. 17, 2018, the United Nations General Assembly approved Global Compact on Refugees 
after two years of extensive consultations conducted by UNHCR with member states, 
international organizations, refugees, civil society, the private sector and experts . One of the 
Compact's four macro goals is precisely to expand access to solutions in third countries by 
developing new pathways or strengthening existing ones.

Project Description 
The university corridors project (University Corridors for Refugees, UNICORE ) aims to promote 
and increase opportunities for refugee students - present in countries of first asylum - to obtain 
study visas and access scholarships in Italy through ad hoc opportunities offered by universities 
and/or higher education organizations and thus be able to complete their studies. 
The objective is to create and consolidate a safe and regular entry model specifically for 
refugees, recognized as such in specific countries of first asylum, already in possession of a 
bachelor's degree, who intend to pursue a university degree (master's degree) in Italy. 
The ultimate goal, however, is not only to guarantee entry into Italy and provide a scholarship 
and other primary services (food, lodging, etc.), but to accompany them on their path of study 
and social inclusion in Italy, in order to contribute to the consolidation of inclusive and 
welcoming societies. 
The project began with a pilot phase in 2019 when 6 students, recognized refugees in Ethiopia, 
were selected to receive a scholarship from the University of Bologna and LUISS University of 
Rome. 
Building on this success, in 2020 10 universities offered 20 scholarships, while in 2021 the number 
of participating universities increased to 24 with an actual offer of 43 scholarships.  
For the latest ongoing edition, UNICORE 6.0, 35 universities have joined the project by providing 
67 scholarships. 
In six years, 42 universities have offered 258 study opportunities to refugee students abroad. 
At the same time, the number of asylum countries involved has also increased, including Kenya, 
Mozambique, Niger, Nigeria, South Africa, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe for the sixth

Beneficiaries of the project are students, recognized refugees in the identified countries of 
asylum, who have completed the first three-year cycle of academic studies - within the five years 
prior to the closing of the UNICORE calls - with a score of 3.0 or higher GPA (Grade Point Average) 
and who are highly motivated to continue their studies in Italy.  
At the local level, the project is supported by the Diocesan Caritas, the territorial branches of the 
Waldensian Diaconia, the  Centro Astalli for Refugees and by associations and entities of various 
nature6, which sign territorial partnership protocols where burdens and tasks  are divided to 
facilitate the inclusion path of the beneficiaries.

Involved subjects  
In addition to the participating universities and UNHCR, national partners of the UNICORE project 
are the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, Caritas Italiana, Diaconia 
Valdese, Centro Astalli per Rifugiati (Italian branch of Jesuit Refugee Service - JRS) and Gandhi 
Charity. 
As of 2022, Banca Etica 12 has signed an agreement to provide support to students admitted to 
the program both by facilitating the opening of bank accounts and by offering scholarships or 
training internships, to be activated during the undergraduate or postgraduate period. 
ACRI, Association of Italian Savings Banks, co-financed the project in the 3.0, 4.0 and 5.0 editions, 
contributing to the purchase of airline tickets , integration activities and health support.  While 
donations received from Calzedonia, Dieci Trust Onlus and Permira supported the

implementation of the project in the fifth and sixth editions. 
Alongside national partners, each university identifies local partners called upon to offer specific 
student support services. 
National and local partners (which include municipalities, NGOs, voluntary associations, banking 
foundations, etc.) then offer each student a range of services and benefits that aim  not only to 
ensure their entry into the territory but also a positive and gradual path to inclusion in Italy. 
A rewarding feature of the project is the centrality of the participating universities, which are 
charged not only with preparing an ad hoc call for applications but also have sole responsibility 
for the selection of applicants based on the academic skills acquired in their previous course of 
study and of their motivation. Moreover, the universities are required to closely follow and 
monitor, together with local partners, the students' course of study and inclusion through 
periodic meetings to intervene, if  necessary, with any corrective measures or more specific 
support actions. 
The project has been further enriched from year to year based on lessons learned from previous 
editions. For example, given the difficulty of students who have arrived in Italy in learning the 
Italian language (also due to the fact that the vast majority of university courses are exclusively 
in English), starting with the third edition , online Italian language courses - in the pre-departure 
phase - have been provided for  the selected students, made available free of charge by the 
University for Foreigners o f Siena and Perugia in recent years (and by the University  of Notre 
Dame for the fourth and fifth editions). It is intended to further enhance language learning by 
encouraging participation in Italian courses on the ground. 
On a regular basis, the national partners organize coordination meetings to get a timely picture 
of the progress of the project, the needs related to the territories and specifically the needs of 
individual students, with the goal of following and monitoring their path to graduation and 
subsequent post -graduation opportunities (stay in Italy with asylum application or 
renewal/conversion of residence permit for study/work; return to the country of first asylum).

Final considerations 
The UNICORE project constitutes a particularly innovative experience in the Italian and European 
landscape. Its strengths include synergy between institutional and non -institutional actors, 
coordination at the central and local levels, and the involvement of different actors. UNICORE 
represents a model to which other European countries are looking at such as Belgium and Ireland 
within the project "EU-Passworld: widening complementary pathways linked to Community 
Sponsorship" financed by the European Commission with AMIF funds and aimed inter alia at the 
expansion of university corridors in Belgium and Ireland. It should also be noted that France has 
launched Corridors UNIV ’R University for Refugees 14 and that in the Netherlands preliminary 
actions are being taken to create Education Pathways on the UNICORE model. 
Moreover, UNICORE was presented by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International 
Cooperation as Italian best practice at the Global Refugee Forum in December 201915 and again 
by the University of Bologna, as the spokesperson for the other universities involved , at the 
second edition in 2023 16. In addition, the program has been the subject of extensive study and 
analysis, so much so that to date 5 theses have been dedicated to it , as well as having been 
presented at numerous Italian and international fora.

Six years after its launch, UNHCR, Caritas Italiana, and Diaconia Valdese are working to improve 
some of the critical issues that emerged during the various project phases, the most relevant of 
which are indicated below. 
The areas on which action is considered necessary are specifically related to: 
⮚ Need to simplify and standardize online application procedures: to make the application 
process easier, following reports from students, significant changes have been made to 
the site and a single, uniform application form has been created for UNICORE 6.0. In 2024, 
the implementation of a new digital platform was initiated, introducing key features 
aimed at enhancing the efficiency and transparency of the process, including: individual 
registration for students, universities, and UNHCR offices; a simplified and accessible 
online application system; timely refugee status verification by UNHCR field Offices; 
management of the selection process by universities; verification of personal data and 
pre-departure arrangements by UNHCR Offices abroad; and regular access to data and 
monitoring by the UNHCR Office in Italy; 
⮚ Student support in pre-registration, visa application and issuance procedures. Students 
reported difficulties in obtaining the Declaration of Value (DoV) required by some 
universities for access and matriculation to the master’s degree Program. A further critical 
issue was found in the acquisition of a declaration of indigence from the Italian authority 
in order to access the facilities offered by the Universities; 
⮚ Streamline travel document issuance operations. Delays have been experienced in the 
issuance of travel documents by the authority of jurisdiction in the country of first asylum; 
in some cases, it was necessary to resort - exceptionally - to the issuance of laissez-passer. 
To ensure that refugees can finalize visa-related procedures in the time strictly necessary, 
the criteria for the selection of countries of departure will continue to include the 
requirement that travel documents are actually available for refugees. Where  students
